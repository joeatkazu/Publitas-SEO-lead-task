# **Publitas Search Performance Assessment & Proposal**

*Joe Robinson · 5 August 2026*

Publitas competes strongly once buyers are already comparing vendors, but it is rarely introduced when buyers first enter the category. Closing that gap could increase primary recommendations across the measured buyer journey by approximately 22–32%, creating a corresponding directional uplift in AI-influenced lead opportunities.

To diagnose the cause, I built a buyer-message mining system and an AI visibility tracker covering 20 buyer-intent prompts across ChatGPT, Claude and Google AI Overviews. Three samples per prompt produced 180 stored answers, including 148 non-branded answers and 738 citations.

The controlled panel was limited to ChatGPT, Claude and Google AI Overviews so that repeated sampling and source capture could be applied consistently. Perplexity and Gemini can be added to the production tracker.

I also audited the 100 most frequently consulted third-party pages, classified every cited domain by ownership, and used DataForSEO data to compare AI visibility with traditional organic demand and rankings.

### Scope

This assessment deliberately focuses on the largest identifiable pipeline constraint: Publitas’s weak visibility when buyers first enter the category.

I also checked for obvious crawler-access restrictions and found none. A full technical audit of templates, indexation, internal linking and Core Web Vitals was outside this diagnostic; it would follow if those factors emerge as material constraints.

## Key Findings

### 1. Publitas performs well once buyers mention it, but AI rarely introduces it

Across the 148 non-branded answers, Publitas was recommended 20 times, more than any other vendor measured. Publitas is visible, and when it appears in consideration, it competes strongly.

However, its performance changes considerably depending on how the buyer asks the question.

When buyers search for alternatives to a vendor they already know, Publitas is the lead recommendation in 22.2% of answers. When buyers simply describe their need without naming any vendor, Publitas leads only 1 of 33 answers, or 3%.

In practical terms, Publitas performs well when it is already part of the buyer’s consideration set, but AI systems rarely introduce it at category entry.

Only 42% of all answers included a clear lead recommendation, making the lead position a distinct and commercially meaningful result rather than a feature of every answer.

Search was forced for ChatGPT and Claude in order to expose citations and fan-out queries, so the retrieval rates below are likely higher than they would be in ordinary consumer sessions.

The broader measure of whether Publitas was mentioned at all shows the same decline across all three engines:

| User behaviour | Group label | ChatGPT | Claude | Google AI search |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Comparing named vendors | Alternatives | 55.6% | 66.7% | 77.8% |
| Describing a problem | Problem-led | 28.6% | 42.9% | 33.3% |
| Entering the category without knowing the vendors | Category entry | 8.3% | 0% | 0% |

The pattern is consistent across systems: Publitas is visible once buyers know the category, but is rarely introduced during initial category discovery.

### 2. There is little third-party category content being cited

At category entry, 69% of citations pulled directly from answers and fan-out queries came from competitors’ own websites. This includes many pages built to look independent. A competitor’s article about the “best flipbook makers” is still competitor-owned content.

Among the 40 third-party pages I could verify, Publitas was absent from 29\. However, 13 of those pages were review platforms, so less straightforward for link or mention placement opportunities.

That leaves 16 pages that could plausibly and credibly add Publitas. Collectively, those addressable editorial pages generated 11 category-entry citations. Across all non-branded queries, they earned 44 citations in total, while publitas.com earned 45\.

This means the independent placement opportunity is small. The available third-party pages have roughly the same citation footprint as Publitas’s own website, rather than several times more.

The independent coverage that does exist is also weak. It consists largely of scraped aggregator pages, a partner page and a student newspaper. None of these acts as a recognised authority for the category.

### 3. Publitas’s organic authority is concentrated in customer catalogues, not category content

Within the top-500-keyword sample, 73.8% of Publitas’s estimated organic traffic goes to `view.publitas.com`, which hosts customers’ own catalogues. By contrast, the pages Publitas controls directly have very little organic traffic.

This creates the appearance of strong performance, but doesn’t help Publitas establish authority in the category from either an SEO or GEO perspective.

Across the 12 main category terms analysed:

- Publitas performs well for searches used to understand the category.  
- It performs poorly for searches used to select a tool.  
- Its blog post ranks ninth for “flipbook”, a term with 14,800 monthly searches.  
- It ranks outside the top 100 for terms representing 84% of the search volume associated with finding a tool.

The `/digital-catalog/` page is one of the few tool-intent pages that does rank: it places seventh in Google for “digital catalog software”. Yet it earned no citations in category-entry AI answers, showing that its current visibility is neither broad enough across commercial search nor strong enough to make it a preferred AI source.

The same asset is therefore failing to secure strong visibility across two important discovery surfaces. This is an example of why SEO and GEO should be treated as one connected category-authority problem, rather than as separate initiatives.

**Findings 2 and 3 point to the same problem:** at category entry, LLMs are citing vendor-written content, but Publitas’s own content is diluted and aimed at buyers who already understand the category.

## Supporting evidence

### The apparent placement list largely disappears on inspection

Some third-party absences initially look like straightforward outreach targets. Fliplink alone accounts for ten cited pages, for example, but it is also a flipbook vendor publishing its own comparison round-ups.

Directory work has a more limited role. Publitas appears on G2, but its low review count leaves it on page two of the Flipbook category and weakens its buyer-validation signals. Because G2 generated no category-entry citations in the sample, improving its review position should be treated as conversion and due-diligence support rather than the primary route into AI shortlists.

Publitas also has a Capterra profile with 58 reviews, but is missing from the relevant category listings while iPaper appears with only five reviews. This points to a category-assignment issue, rather than a review-volume problem.

**So what:** the pool of credible, obtainable editorial placements is small. Capterra taxonomy should be corrected as a low-cost visibility fix, while G2 investment should be assessed primarily as buyer due diligence and conversion support, not as the route into AI shortlists.

### What the evidence rules out

The assessment found no obvious crawler-access restriction: Publitas does not block GPTBot, ClaudeBot or Google-Extended.

Publitas is present on Capterra, SourceForge and G2, so basic directory presence is not the issue. Its content is citeable once retrieved, with an 85% fetch-to-citation rate. It also already has a substantial “X versus Y” comparison library that earns citations.

The remaining diagnosis is therefore one of targeting. Publitas has built effective content for buyers comparing known vendors, but not enough content for buyers entering the category for the first time.

## Proposal

### Objective

Increase qualified leads from organic and AI search by making Publitas more likely to be introduced and recommended when buyers first enter the category.

The primary mechanism is to strengthen the pages Publitas owns, supported by selective investment in video, community participation and a small number of external placements.

### Projected commercial impact
Closing the category-entry visibility gap would increase the number of primary Publitas recommendations across the measured buyer journey by approximately 22–32%.

Because these recommendations represent occasions on which Publitas enters or leads a buyer’s shortlist, the model indicates a corresponding directional increase in AI-influenced lead opportunities. If the current relationship between shortlist inclusion and demo conversion remains broadly stable, this should produce a similar proportional uplift in leads attributable to the measured AI discovery segment.

The effect is considerably larger within category-entry searches themselves. Publitas currently leads 3.0% of these answers; increasing that rate towards the 16.5–22.2% achieved in later-stage prompts would represent a roughly five- to sevenfold increase from the present baseline.

These figures describe the measured AI opportunity, rather than the total expected return from the programme. They exclude additional leads from improved traditional search rankings, international demand, branded and direct return visits, and conversion improvements arising from clearer commercial content.
Week-one attribution work should establish Publitas’s actual recommendation-to-lead and lead-to-SQL rates, so that the percentage opportunity can subsequently be translated into a reliable pipeline and revenue forecast.

### Priority 1: Build category-entry authority on Publitas.com

Vendor-owned pages account for most category-entry citations, but Publitas currently earns only one. The largest opportunity is therefore on Publitas’s own website.

The issue is not simply a lack of content. Publitas already has substantial assets, but many are fragmented, aimed at buyers further down the funnel or missing the clear product information that engines tend to cite.

The first-party programme should focus on four areas.

**Consolidate the accessibility content.** Accessibility is the largest category-entry topic in the study, generating 41 citations across competitor and third-party pages. Publitas already has the relevant product pages, articles, documentation and VPAT, but they are split across several URLs and do not directly answer the buyer’s selection question. These assets should be consolidated, retitled around the language buyers use and repositioned as a guide to selecting an accessible publishing platform.

**Publish a category-level roundup.** Publitas has a mature library of competitor-comparison pages, but these serve buyers who already know which vendors to compare. It needs a ranked category page for buyers asking which digital catalogue or flipbook tools they should consider. Competitor-owned roundups dominate this part of the market, while Publitas currently has almost no share.

**Improve the digital catalogue product page.** The `/digital-catalog/` page ranks in traditional search but earns no category-entry citations. It should be strengthened with specific product facts, including limits, file sizes, plan details, pricing context and a concise description that can be quoted independently. This work should improve both organic rankings and AI visibility.

**Expand problem-led documentation.** Publitas documentation appeared in only one of 101 retrieval sets, compared with 22 for Flipsnack. New and revised documentation should address the practical questions buyers ask, particularly how to update a catalogue without changing its link or retagging every product. This is an existing product strength that is not yet visible in search.

### Priority 2: Develop the strongest off-page opportunities

Off-page investment should be selective because the pool of credible, obtainable placements is small.

**Expand Publitas’s existing YouTube channel around the six video topics already earning category-entry citations.** YouTube videos generated a separate set of 11 category-entry citations across the prompt panel, yet Publitas’s existing videos did not appear in the cited results. The priority is therefore not channel creation, but publishing and optimising content around the topics already shown to influence category-entry answers.

**Participate openly in relevant Reddit discussions.** Reddit contributes five category-entry citations, particularly around accessibility. Participation should be useful, transparent and led by subject expertise rather than promotion.

**Limit editorial outreach to credible, reachable pages.** Independent editorial sources generated 17 citations, but only five came from pages where Publitas was absent. Outreach should concentrate on this small addressable set rather than a broad placement campaign. 

**Note:** Broader digital PR may still support brand demand and mental availability, but this dataset does not justify it as the first programme to fund.

**Treat G2 as a buyer-validation channel.** A larger review base may strengthen due diligence and visibility in “best software” content, but G2 currently generates no category-entry citations. The review programme should therefore support conversion and credibility, not serve as the main discovery tactic.

### Prioritised plan

| Priority | Action | Business rationale | Effort | Primary measure |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 1 | Establish the attribution baseline | Connects visibility changes to qualified leads before implementation alters the baseline | Small | AI-assisted lead share and SQL rate |
| 2 | Improve `/digital-catalog/` | Already ranks in Google but earns no category-entry citations | Small | First-party citations, organic rank and conversion |
| 3 | Consolidate and reposition accessibility content | Largest category-entry topic; Publitas already owns the necessary assets | Medium | Category-entry retrieval and recommendation rate |
| 4 | Publish a category-level roundup | Competitor-owned roundups dominate vendor citations | Medium | Recommendations and tool-intent rankings |
| 5 | Expand problem-led documentation | Publitas documentation is rarely retrieved despite relevant product strengths | Medium | Documentation retrieval and citation rate |
| 6 | Produce category-entry videos and pursue creator partnerships | YouTube is the largest unworked third-party opportunity | Large | Video citations and category-entry recommendation rate |
| 7 | Conduct selective editorial outreach and Reddit participation | Only a small, evidenced set of external opportunities is addressable | Small | Qualified placements and third-party citation share |

The citation figures overlap across topics and source types, so they should not be added together as a single opportunity total.

Pricing and small-business page improvements, Capterra reassignment and G2 review growth should remain secondary. They may improve conversion and buyer due diligence, but this analysis does not identify them as the principal category-entry constraint.

Broad outreach to competitor-owned roundups and further competitor-comparison pages should be excluded. The existing comparison programme is mature and already performs well.

## Measurement and operating model

### Establish attribution before work begins

AI influence cannot be measured reliably through referral traffic alone. Buyers may discover Publitas in an assistant, then return later through branded search, direct traffic or another channel.

Measurement should therefore begin in week one, before content or outreach changes the baseline.

| Stage | Metric | Current baseline |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Visibility | Recommendations at category entry | 1 in 33 |
| Visibility | Publitas citations at category entry | 1 |
| Influence | Share of new leads reporting AI-assisted discovery | Not captured |
| Demand | Branded search impressions and direct visits | Not baselined |
| Revenue | SQL rate from branded organic and direct traffic | Not measurable today |

The primary commercial question at day 90 is whether inbound leads are becoming better qualified. Recommendation and citation growth are KPIs but SQL rate is the outcome.

Branded and direct traffic should be treated as directional evidence rather than clean AI attribution. Lead-source self-reporting, Search Console trends and CRM conversion data should be assessed together.

### Use automation for monitoring and prioritisation

The system should automate repetitive research, while strategic judgement and final content production remain human-led.

The system will:

* Re-run the prompt panel weekly.  
* Track changes in recommendations, citations and retrieved sources.  
* Identify newly cited third-party pages where Publitas is absent.  
* Identify buyer questions where competitors’ content is cited but Publitas lacks an effective equivalent.  
* Rank opportunities by demonstrated citation value.

Content briefs can draw on the existing evidence library, but all recommendations, drafts and publication decisions should remain subject to human review.

The full system is reproducible: the stored answers, prompt set, tracker configuration and analysis scripts remain available in the repository https://github.com/joeatkazu/Publitas-SEO-lead-task.

### Quarterly operating cadence

| Workstream | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Measurement | Establish GA4 and CRM baselines; add lead self-reporting | Review early signals | Reassess visibility, demand and SQL quality |
| Content | Repair `/digital-catalog/`; plan accessibility consolidation | Rebuild accessibility and other category-entry assets | Measure effect on retrieval and citation, iterate |
| Off-page | Begin editorial outreach and Reddit participation | Publish category-entry videos and pursue creator partnerships | Track placements, citations and recommendations |
| Reviews | Define G2 review programme | Begin review collection | Assess due-diligence and best-of visibility |
| Monitoring | Start weekly reporting and prioritisation | Refresh opportunity queues | Set the next-quarter plan |

Month one should combine measurement with quick, high-confidence work. Selective outreach can begin immediately because it may produce faster feedback, but first-party improvements should run in parallel rather than wait until month two.

By the end of the quarter, the decision is whether category-entry visibility improved, whether more buyers entered the funnel and whether those leads converted into SQLs at a higher rate.

### Resourcing

The programme requires one accountable owner, access to existing analytics and CRM data, and support from content, development, video and outreach resources. No additional monitoring software is required.

## Appendix: Methodology and systems built

This assessment was produced through a repeatable system designed to identify how buyers describe the category, track how AI engines respond, and convert those findings into a prioritised marketing programme.

### Buyer-message mining

A message-mining system was built to extract buyer language, product strengths, switching triggers and objections from Publitas and competitor reviews.

These signals were combined with category terminology, Google search behaviour and community discussions to create a panel of 20 buyer-intent prompts. The panel covers buyers comparing known vendors, describing a problem and entering the category without knowing which products exist.

This ensures the research reflects buyer language rather than terminology chosen internally by Publitas.

### AI visibility tracker

The 20 prompts were tested three times across ChatGPT, Claude and Google AI Overviews, producing 180 stored answers.

The tracker records:

* Which vendors are mentioned and recommended.  
* Which vendor receives the lead recommendation.  
* Which pages are consulted and cited.  
* How results differ by buyer intent and engine.  
* The evidence passage supporting each classification.

The complete answers and source data are retained, so findings can be re-analysed without repeating the original tests. The panel can also be rerun weekly to track changes in recommendations, citations and source selection.

**Implementation stack.** I used Claude Code to build and iterate the Python orchestration and analysis scripts, with the DataForSEO API, structured answer and citation storage, and LLM-assisted classification supported by retained evidence passages. All findings and recommendations remained subject to human review.

### Opportunity prioritisation

The tracker feeds two working queues.

The **on-page queue** identifies buyer questions where competitors’ product or support content is cited but Publitas lacks an effective equivalent, or has a relevant page that engines are not selecting.

The **off-page queue** identifies cited third-party pages where Publitas is absent. Each source is classified by ownership so that competitor-owned roundups are removed from the outreach list and opportunities are ranked by demonstrated citation value.

### Human review and self-correction

Automation supports monitoring, classification and prioritisation. Strategic interpretation, content approval and publication remain human-led.

The system also checks its own conclusions across multiple evidence sources. Several initial findings were overturned during the assessment: Publitas was found to be present in directories where automated checks suggested it was absent; accessibility proved to be a positioning and consolidation problem rather than a lack of content; and many apparent editorial targets were revealed to be competitor-owned.

This self-correcting process is important. The objective is not to preserve the first diagnosis, but to retain the evidence and revise the recommendation when a stronger explanation emerges.

### Reproducibility

The stored corpus, prompt panel, tracker configuration, source classifications and analysis scripts are retained in the project repository https://github.com/joeatkazu/Publitas-SEO-lead-task. Every principal figure in the report can therefore be regenerated, challenged and monitored over time.

The panel is a controlled diagnostic and tracking system, rather than a direct measurement of total consumer AI demand. Commercial impact should ultimately be assessed through Search Console, analytics and CRM data.
